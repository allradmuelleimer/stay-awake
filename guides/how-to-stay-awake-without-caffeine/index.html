How to stay awake without caffeine

Without caffeine you have to use other signals: bright light, cool air, movement and something to do. None of it works miracles, and each signal only lasts a short while – so what counts in the end is the rhythm in which you repeat them.

10 ways to try

  1. Bright light. Main light on; in the morning go into daylight.
  2. Cool air. Open the window, turn the heating down a little.
  3. Cold water on your face, wrists and neck.
  4. Stand up. Work standing for a while, at a counter or shelf.
  5. Move briefly. Stairs, a walk round the block, a few squats.
  6. Sit upright, feet on the floor – not curled up on the sofa.
  7. Chew something: gum, an apple, raw vegetables.
  8. Talk. A call, a conversation, explaining something out loud.
  9. Music with a beat, if your surroundings allow it.
  10. Change tasks when one starts to make you drowsy.

Rhythm beats willpower

Each of these works for a few minutes. What carries you through hours is a rhythm: every 20 to 30 minutes a small action that interrupts the drift. Set it from outside – a timer, an appointment, a reminder – because the drowsier you are, the less you notice the time.

Honestly

Nothing replaces sleep. If you are so tired that none of this helps, your body is telling you something – and the best decision may be to sleep.

Questions and answers

What is the quickest way to wake up without coffee?

For many people: stand up, cold water on the face, a few minutes of fresh air or movement.

Do energy drinks count as caffeine-free?

No, most energy drinks contain caffeine. Check the label.

Why do I get tired again after a few minutes?

Most of these tricks work only briefly. That is why a regular rhythm of small interruptions helps more than one big effort.
